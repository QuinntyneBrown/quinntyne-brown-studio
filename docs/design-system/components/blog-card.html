Blog card

A blog card is a single article in a listing: image, date, title and a two-line abstract, all one link to the post.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableWhole-card link

Overview

Use blog cards on the blog index, in search results and as related articles. For other content with a heading and text use a card; for photographs use a photo tile. Lay cards out with the grid: one column on phones, two or three above.

  • The whole card is one link; the title is its accessible name.
  • The image is decorative in a link already named by the title, so its alt may be empty when the article image adds nothing.
  • An article without an image drops the image block; the text moves up.

Anatomy

  1. Image16:10 crop with alt; optional.
  2. Date<time datetime> in overline type.
  3. Titleh2 in the serif display style; underlined on hover and focus.
  4. AbstractTwo or three lines of muted body-sm text.

Variants

The card is the same everywhere; the grid around it changes.

  • Visited: the title keeps its colour; do not rely on visited styling.
  • Image failed: the frame keeps its ratio on --color-bg-subtle and the card remains usable.
  • Loading: use skeleton blocks for the image, a short line for the date and two lines for the title.
  • No results: replace the grid with an empty state offering to clear the search.

Responsive behaviour

  • One column below 768px, two from 768px and three from 1100px using the layout grid. Cards keep the same internal layout at every width.
  • The image keeps a 16:10 ratio and crops rather than distorting.
  • Titles wrap to as many lines as they need; abstracts clamp visually to the author's two sentences and are never truncated with an ellipsis in code.
  • The date line and the title never overlap on 320px screens.

Theming

Title and date use the default and muted foregrounds; the image frame uses --color-bg-subtle. The link colour is inherited so the card does not look like a blue link in either theme.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

One native <a> wrapping the content of an <article>. Because the link contains a heading, the link name is its full text content; keep the abstract short so the name is not verbose, or move the link onto the heading and stretch it over the card with a pseudo-element. See the APG names and descriptions guidance.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves to the card link and out.
EnterOpens the article.
EscNo effect.

Focus

The shared 2px ring appears around the link, which encloses the whole card. Do not remove the outline. Heading order: card titles are h2 under a page h1.

Labelling

The link is named by the date, title and abstract together; if that is too long, give the link aria-labelledby pointing to the title. Image alt describes the photograph, or is empty when it only repeats the title. The date uses <time datetime>.

Motion

Hover only underlines the title. There is no image zoom; it would shift content for motion-sensitive users.

Touch

The whole card is the target and far exceeds 44px. Keep at least 16px between cards.

Content

  • Titles are sentence case, specific and under about 70 characters: "How to plan a wedding timeline".
  • Abstracts are one or two sentences, about 25 words, and add information not in the title.
  • Dates are long form with the month spelled out: "14 October 2026".
  • Do not write "Read more" links; the card is the link.
  • Images show the subject of the article, not decoration.
