Footer

The footer closes every public page with the brand, a short list of secondary links and the copyright line.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableLandmark <footer>

Overview

Use the footer on the marketing and client sites. It is a safety net, not a second menu: it repeats the destinations people look for last (About, Contact, Client access) and carries legal text. The admin workspace has no footer; the sidebar holds its secondary links.

  • Simple (the studio's footer): brand and a line of place, a row of links, and the copyright. All three wrap.
  • Multi-column: the same parts with the link row split into labelled groups when the site grows. Compose it with the layout primitives; the footer itself keeps one row.

Anatomy

  1. Brand and lineWordmark and one sentence about where the studio works.
  2. Link row<nav aria-label="Footer"> of plain links; wraps on narrow screens.
  3. CopyrightSmall print at the end, © year and the studio name.

Variants

Both variants use .site-footer. The multi-column form nests labelled lists inside the nav.

Sizes

The footer has one size; its height follows its content with padding from the spacing scale.

States

The footer has no states of its own. Its links have default, focus and current (when the footer repeats the page's own link). Hover underlines are not defined; links are distinguished by colour and position, so keep them in a labelled nav.

  • Current page: do not mark footer links with aria-current; the top bar already does.
  • Empty: if a product has no secondary links, the footer shows only brand and copyright.

Responsive behaviour

  • The three parts sit in one row and wrap: below about 700px the link row drops under the brand and the copyright drops under that.
  • Padding is --layout-margin on the sides, so it lines up with the top bar and the .container.
  • At 390px the links wrap into lines with 24px gaps; each is still at least 24px tall.
  • The bottom padding (48px) keeps the last line clear of browser UI on phones.

Theming

The footer uses --color-border-default for the top rule, --color-fg-muted for the small print and --color-fg-default for links. All flip with the theme; nothing else is needed.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A page-level <footer> is the contentinfo landmark. Its link list is a <nav aria-label="Footer"> so it appears in the landmark list separate from the main navigation. APG: Contentinfo landmark.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the brand and footer links in order.
EnterFollows the focused link.

Focus

Links use the shared ring (2px --color-focus-ring, 2px offset). The footer is the last stop in the tab order of the page.

Labelling

Only one <footer> that is a direct child of <body> becomes a landmark; a <footer> inside an article or card does not. The nav needs its own label; the brand link's text is its name.

Touch

Footer links are text-height targets with 24px between them, meeting the 24px minimum. In touch-first views add 8px of block padding to each link to approach 44px.

Content

  • Links are the same names used in the top bar: "About the studio", "Get in touch", "Our work".
  • Keep it to six or fewer links; more means the main navigation is incomplete.
  • The line under the brand names a place, not a slogan.
  • Copyright reads "© 2026 Quinntyne Brown Studio"; update the year with the build.
  • Do not put the call to action in the footer on its own; repeat it where the content asks for it.
