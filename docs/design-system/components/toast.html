Toast

A toast confirms something the person just did without taking them out of the task: photographs added, selection saved, changes discarded. It appears bottom right, stays long enough to read, and goes away on its own unless it reports a problem.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableLive region

Overview

Use a toast for short, low-stakes confirmation of an action that has already succeeded and needs no decision. Use an alert when the message must stay on the page, an inline message when it belongs to one field, and a dialog when the person must decide something. See notifications for choosing between them.

  • Message only is the default: one sentence in the past tense or a plain statement.
  • With action adds one button such as Undo or View. The action must also be reachable some other way, because the toast will disappear.
  • Error uses role="alert", never disappears by itself and has a dismiss button. If the person has to fix something, use an alert or inline message instead.
  • Stacked toasts queue: the newest appears at the bottom and older ones move up. Show at most three; further messages replace the oldest.

Timing rule

  • A success or information toast stays for at least 8 seconds, plus one second per 20 words beyond the first 20.
  • The timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast or focus is inside it, and restarts at the full duration when the pointer leaves or focus moves away.
  • An error toast persists until dismissed or until the problem is resolved. A toast with an action also persists while the action is focused.
  • With prefers-reduced-motion the toast still appears and disappears, without sliding.

The mock's 5-second timer is too short for WCAG 2.2.1 (Timing Adjustable) and for slower readers; the system raises it to 8 seconds and adds the pause.

Anatomy

  1. MessageOne sentence, sentence case, ending with a full stop. It is read aloud by screen readers when it appears.
  2. Action (optional)A single small ghost button in the inverse colours, such as Undo. Never more than one.
  3. Dismiss (optional, required for errors)Icon-only ghost button with aria-label. Container: inverse surface, --shadow-3, --radius-xs, --z-toast.

Sizes

A toast has one size. Its width follows the message up to 26rem; it never grows to fit a paragraph.

States

A toast has no hover, focus or disabled styling of its own. What changes is its timer: moving the pointer onto it or focus into it pauses the countdown. data-state="paused" marks that state in static pages.

  • Empty: the live region stays in the DOM with no content (.toast:empty is hidden) so the first message is announced reliably.

Responsive behaviour

  • Above 768px the toast sits --space-6 from the bottom and right edges, up to 26rem wide.
  • Below 768px it spans the viewport minus the gutters and sits above any fixed bottom bar such as the selection bar, never over it.
  • Messages that wrap to more than three lines at 390px are too long; shorten them or use an alert.
  • Stacks collapse to the newest two toasts on small screens.

Theming

The toast uses the inverse pair (--color-bg-inverse and --color-fg-inverse) so it stands out from the page in both themes: a dark toast on the light theme, a light toast on the dark theme. Embedded buttons are re-mapped through --btn-fg and --btn-border to the inverse colours, and the focus ring switches to the inverse foreground.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

WAI-ARIA live region. A persistent container with role="status" and aria-live="polite" announces confirmations; errors use role="alert" (assertive). There is no APG widget pattern for toasts, and a toast is not a dialog: focus is never moved to it.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabReaches the action and dismiss buttons in DOM order. The toast is not a focus trap and does not take focus on appearing.
Enter, SpaceActivates the focused action or dismiss button.
EscDismisses the toast that holds focus, then returns focus to where it was.
F6 (optional)Moves between the page and the notification region when a stack is present.

Focus

Focus stays where it was. If focus is inside a toast when it is removed, return it to the element that last had focus outside the region. The pause-on-focus rule means a keyboard user always has time to reach an action.

Labelling

The live region is announced as its content changes, so keep the container in the DOM and replace its text. The dismiss button has aria-label="Dismiss notification". Do not rely on the leading icon or colour to convey tone; say "failed" or "saved" in words.

Motion

Enter and exit are short fades with an 8px rise; under prefers-reduced-motion they become instant. No toast auto-dismisses faster than 8 seconds, which satisfies 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable together with the pause on hover and focus.

Touch

Action and dismiss buttons are small (32px) because they sit inside a 56px toast with 8px between them; on touch devices raise them to the 44px medium size.

Content

  • One sentence. State what happened, not what the system did: "Photographs added to Wedding session.", not "Operation completed successfully."
  • Errors say what happened and what is safe: "Some files need another try. Completed photographs are safe."
  • Action labels are one word or a short verb phrase: Undo, View, Retry.
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji, no jargon, and no message that merely repeats a button the person just pressed.
