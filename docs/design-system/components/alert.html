Alert

An alert is a message that stays on the page until the situation changes: a failed upload, a pending review, a service problem. The mocks call it a notice.

Overview

Use an alert for information the person needs while they work, and that must still be there if they look away. Use a toast for a passing confirmation, an inline message for one field, and a dialog when they must decide before continuing.

  • Info (the default) gives neutral context: "Photographs are processed within a few minutes."
  • Success confirms a completed outcome that stays relevant, such as "3 print requests sent."
  • Warning flags something that may go wrong or needs attention soon.
  • Danger reports a failure or a blocked task. It is the only tone that may use role="alert" on page load.

An alert can sit inline in a section or span the top of the page as a banner. It can carry one action, and it can be dismissed when it is not critical.

Anatomy

  1. IconRepeats the tone for people who cannot rely on colour. aria-hidden.
  2. MessageOne or two sentences. States what happened and, for warnings and errors, what to do.
  3. Action (optional)One secondary or ghost small button, such as Retry or View details.
  4. Dismiss (optional)Icon-only ghost button. Never on a danger alert that blocks a task. Container: tone background, border and foreground tokens.

Variants

Four tones share one structure. Colour is never the only signal: each carries an icon and words.

Sizes

An alert fills the width of its container and has one density. Its height follows its content.

States

The alert itself is not interactive, so it has no hover, focus or pressed styling. Its buttons carry the standard button states. What varies is the tone and whether it can be dismissed.

  • Appearing: inserted without animation so the live-region announcement is not delayed.
  • Dismissed: removed from the DOM and focus moves to the next focusable element, or to the page heading if there is none.
  • Loading and disabled do not apply: an alert reports state, it does not have its own.

Responsive behaviour

  • The alert fills its container. Below 768px, the row layout wraps: the message takes the full line and the action moves beneath it, left-aligned.
  • Banners span the full content width and sit above the page heading at every size.
  • At 390px keep the message to two lines; longer explanation belongs in the page body with the alert linking to it.

Theming

Each tone is built from four tokens: background, border, foreground and (for solid uses elsewhere) solid. In the dark theme the backgrounds become very dark tints of the hue and the text and border lighten, so the text pair stays above 7:1. Info uses the olive brand tint, not blue, so the tone belongs to the studio's palette.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

WAI-ARIA alert role for new, important messages (failures); status role for polite messages. A static alert present at page load needs no live role at all. There is no keyboard interaction on the alert itself. See the APG Alert pattern.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves to the action and dismiss buttons in DOM order. The alert is not itself focusable.
Enter, SpaceActivates the focused action or dismiss button.
EscNo effect; alerts are not modal.

Focus

Focus is never moved to an alert when it appears, except after a failed form submission, where focus moves to the error summary. When a dismissed alert is removed, move focus to the next focusable element or the page heading.

Labelling

The message is the accessible content; no extra label is needed. Use role="alert" only for content that appears after load and needs immediate attention, otherwise role="status" or no role. Dismiss buttons say what they dismiss: aria-label="Dismiss message".

Motion

No motion. Alerts appear and disappear instantly so announcements are not delayed.

Touch

Action and dismiss buttons are small (32px) with 16px of padding around them; on touch devices raise them to 44px or give them a 44px hit area.

Content

  • Lead with what happened, then what to do: "We could not save this session. Try again."
  • Name the thing: "Two photographs failed to upload", not "Some items failed".
  • Do not blame the person: "That date is unavailable", not "You entered an invalid date".
  • Sentence case, a full stop at the end, no exclamation marks. Keep to two lines.
